## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this Evidence Pack. Based on the repurposing rationale supplied, mepolizumab is an anti-IL-5 monoclonal antibody that suppresses eosinophil proliferation and activity, and is approved for eosinophil-driven conditions such as hypereosinophilic syndrome (HES).

The link to thrombocytopenia due to immune destruction is indirect: some HES patients develop concurrent immune-mediated platelet destruction as part of a broader dysregulated immune state. Suppressing eosinophil-driven inflammation could theoretically dampen this associated immune dysregulation, but mepolizumab has no known direct mechanism acting on platelet immune clearance pathways (e.g., anti-platelet antibodies or Fc-receptor-mediated destruction).

The single supporting case report describes resolution of a steroid-resistant hypereosinophilic process together with amelioration of a concurrent thrombotic microangiopathy after mepolizumab treatment — a single, mechanistically indirect observation rather than a targeted demonstration of anti-thrombocytopenic effect. This is consistent with the model's L4 (preclinical/mechanism-only) evidence rating and "Research Question" designation.
