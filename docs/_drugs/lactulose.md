## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed formal mechanism-of-action data for lactulose is flagged as a data gap in this evidence pack. However, the literature evidence collected here consistently documents lactulose's known pharmacology as a non-absorbable disaccharide: colonic bacterial fermentation acidifies the gut lumen, which reduces ammonia and endotoxin absorption. This mechanism underlies its established use in hepatic encephalopathy (explicitly noted in PMID 9145459) and is the basis for the repurposing hypothesis in obstructive jaundice.

Obstructive jaundice is frequently complicated by gut bacterial translocation and portal/systemic endotoxaemia, which in turn drives postoperative renal dysfunction in patients undergoing biliary surgery. Because lactulose's endotoxin-reducing effect is mechanistically identical regardless of the underlying liver condition, multiple research groups tested whether preoperative lactulose could reduce endotoxaemia and protect renal function in obstructive jaundice patients — this is the exact rationale captured in the evidence pack's TxGNN repurposing note.

Importantly, the evidence is mixed: some studies (e.g., PMID 2032107, a multicentre RCT) support a protective effect on postoperative renal function, while others (e.g., PMID 2614579) found no benefit on hepatic injury endpoints. The predicted indication is therefore best understood as *perioperative renal protection in obstructive jaundice*, not a direct treatment of jaundice itself — this distinction matters for how the evidence should be interpreted.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The strongest evidence supports lactulose for *perioperative renal protection in obstructive jaundice surgery* (L2, one completed multicentre RCT), not for treating jaundice as a disease — and findings across studies are mixed (some positive on endotoxaemia/renal endpoints, one negative on hepatic injury). Combined with the drug's unmarketed status in Saudi Arabia and missing MOA/safety data, this does not yet meet the bar for a Go or Proceed-with-Guardrails decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Formal DrugBank MOA data (DG002)
- SFDA/TFDA package insert warnings and contraindications (DG001 — currently a Blocking gap for safety review)
- A dedicated trial (or updated systematic review) confirming benefit specifically for perioperative renal protection in obstructive jaundice, given the conflicting evidence found (PMID 2032107 positive vs. PMID 2614579 negative)
- Saudi Arabia regulatory pathway assessment, since the drug is not currently marketed there
- Clarification of the target indication label (perioperative renal protection vs. "obstructive jaundice" as a disease) before any clinical positioning is finalized
