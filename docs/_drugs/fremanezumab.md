# Fremanezumab: From Migraine Prevention to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Fremanezumab is an anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody originally developed for the preventive treatment of migraine (episodic and chronic migraine). The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, a rare migraine subtype, with **99.94% prediction confidence**. Currently no dedicated clinical trials exist for this specific subtype; supporting evidence is limited to **20 publications**, mostly preclinical/mechanistic studies and case reports/series in related aura subtypes (e.g., hemiplegic migraine).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, a detailed mechanism of action record from DrugBank is not available (data gap). Based on the evidence pack's repurposing rationale, fremanezumab is a fully humanized IgG2Δa monoclonal antibody that selectively targets calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), a key mediator of trigeminovascular system activation in migraine pathophysiology.

Migraine with brainstem aura (formerly "basilar-type migraine") involves aura symptoms believed to originate from cortical/brainstem spreading depression (CSD). Since fremanezumab's original approved use — migraine prevention — already targets the CGRP pathway central to migraine pathophysiology broadly, the mechanistic extension to an aura-predominant subtype is biologically plausible.

However, the evidence pack notes an important caveat: preclinical studies specifically testing fremanezumab's effect on CSD (an animal model of aura) found that fremanezumab did **not** prevent CSD occurrence, though it may modulate related vascular responses. Most clinical literature available concerns general migraine populations (including chronic migraine) rather than the brainstem aura subtype specifically; only one publication directly reviews anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody effects on migraine aura via case reports.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence is currently mechanism-level (L4) — supported mainly by preclinical CSD-model studies and case reports/series in related aura subtypes, with no clinical trials or literature specific to "migraine with brainstem aura." One preclinical study found fremanezumab did not prevent CSD occurrence, which tempers the mechanistic rationale. Additionally, regulatory and safety data are blocked by a critical data gap (DG001: SFDA/TFDA package insert warnings and contraindications unavailable), preventing safety pre-screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SFDA/TFDA official package insert (warnings, contraindications) — required to clear the S1 safety gate (DG001)
- Detailed DrugBank mechanism of action record (DG002)
- A dedicated clinical study or subgroup analysis in migraine-with-brainstem-aura or broader migraine-with-aura populations
- Clarification of fremanezumab's registration/import pathway status in Saudi Arabia, given it is currently unmarketed
